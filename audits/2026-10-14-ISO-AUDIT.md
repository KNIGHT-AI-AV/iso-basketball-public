# ISO: Basketball — audit, public edition

Audit dated October 14, 2026 · Public edition prepared when the project was retired and published

This is an audit record, not a design document. It adds no requirement and changes none of the design documents or catalogues. The original audit also scored feasibility and timelines against the original team's private resources, staffing, schedule and other commitments. Those team-specific parts are omitted here. Everything below applies to anyone considering the project. The restricted basketball reference product is not named; one competitor row refers to it generically.

## Outcome for the original team

The original team graded ISO below its green-light bar and retired the project. The decisive issues were (1) building the full vision needs specialist animation, character and cloth work that the team did not have, and (2) there is no demand evidence yet for this specific game. A better-resourced builder may reach a different answer; section 7 describes a small first test that would produce that evidence.

Proposed priorities: 47 P1, 107 P2, 57 P3. Coverage sheet: 10 parent groups + 41 cases = 51 rows; parent candidate counts sum to 215; every move points to a valid parent.
- **School catalogue: 20 fictional high schools plus 20 fictional colleges** (one of each per state, 20 states). There is no professional-team catalogue; the professional league is a single name-pending slot ("Ultimate Basketball Association / UBA" is a candidate with existing-use concerns).

## 3. Findings on the plan itself

- **The quality requirements compound.** Faces beyond the references, real fabric response, a 120 fps target, six-character scenes, and layered 2.5D scenery are each plausible alone. Together they are a professional-studio target, and the dossier never estimates the combination.
- **The main camera reference shrinks the player at the rim.** The reel is shot from a fixed position. The player is about 40% of frame height at the start and roughly 8% at the rim (estimated from sampled frames). Under Wide Throughout, dunks, hands and fabric will read very small. Visible quality matters most in close views; spend there first.
- **The character references set a modest floor.** Both are mobile soccer-game screenshots with generic faces and painted wrinkles. Surpassing them is routine for a skilled character artist.
- **Gate 1 is not small.** [Document 08](../docs/08-FUTURE-MVP-AND-EVALUATION.md) bundles roughly eighteen action families, both cameras, haptics, stamina, defense practice, airborne conversion, and the face/fabric floor. A genuine first validation needs to be narrower (section 7).
- **Paid conversion conflicts with fair PvP.** Converting Style Credits to stat-buying Coins is paid progression, which undercuts future competitive play. Still unresolved.
- **Starter outfit.** The white tank and gray joggers match the clothing in the main reference reel. Generic clothing is fine; the character must not be modeled on the person in the reel.

## 4. Build risks for anyone picking this up

| Area | Risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gameplay code and systems | Moderate | Gesture recognition, possession states, one authoritative ball and timestamped events are conventional engineering; game feel still needs on-device iteration |
| Animation and ball/hand/foot contact | High | The critical path. 212 candidate rows plus transitions, 3v3 passing and lobs. Markerless capture gives body drafts; ball contact, fingers and foot plants need deliberate cleanup |
| Faces and fabric at the stated floor | High | Licensing for high-quality faces is available (below); mobile runtime cost of faces plus cloth on six characters at 120 fps is unmeasured |
| Device performance | High | 120 fps with cloth and six characters is unmeasured; a 1v1 scene at 60 fps is ordinary |
| Content volume | High | 40 school identities, venue kits, a broad wardrobe, tattoos and shoes |
| Store and purchases | Moderate | Standard platform purchase flows, a durable ledger and entitlements |
| Live PvP | High | Authoritative timing, prediction, reconnect, anti-cheat and operations |

### Evidence that would change this view

- Strangers voluntarily replaying a gray-box build, measured rather than reported.
- A capture and cleanup path producing game-ready dribbling with correct ball contact.
- A demonstrated way to reach basketball players organically.
- A competitor shipping portrait swipe-dribbling (either proving demand or raising the bar).

## 7. A small first validation stage

The smallest test that answers the two open questions: does swipe dribbling feel good enough that people want another possession, and can the motion be made clean?

- **Build:** a phone gray box with a stand-in character; crossover, between-legs, hesitation, in-and-out and retreat dribbles; a drive, a stop, a fail/okay/perfect standing jumper, one two-hand dunk; a scripted defender with visible commitment; wide camera only; shot clock; a log of intended versus recognized gestures. No economy, art pass, cloth, PvP or store work.
- **Limits:** set a fixed time and money cap before starting.
- **Pass, all required:** fewer than 5% wrong accepted actions on single and chained gestures; at least 90% of 10–15 first-time players perform crossover → drive → shot after a short tutorial; most testers start additional possessions unprompted (counted); no visible foot sliding or ball teleporting at gameplay distance; 60 fps sustained for 20 minutes, with the 120 fps result recorded where the device supports it.
- **Stop:** if voluntary replay or the motion test fails within the cap, stop.
- **Limits of a pass:** it does not prove the face and fabric floor, the commercial model, or demand at scale.
