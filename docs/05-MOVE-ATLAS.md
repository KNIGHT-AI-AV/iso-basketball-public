# Move atlas and animation coverage

The accompanying CSV is a first-pass design inventory. It uses plain basketball descriptions and our proposed production categories. It does not contain a copied animation database, player packages, proprietary ratings, or source control mappings.

## What an entry means

A **family** is a type of basketball action. A **variation** changes its mechanics or context. A **sequence** combines actions. A **reaction** is a response to another action. A **style** changes execution character without necessarily creating a new technique.

Examples: a crossover is a family; a low crossover is a variation; crossover into retreat is a sequence; a defender stumbling is a reaction. A named player's manner of doing a crossover is a style reference, not automatic permission to reproduce their likeness or captured animation.

All catalogue rows are design candidates requiring movement review. The `review_status` field does not assert that choreography has been verified or that an animation exists. Priority indicates a possible future scope, not a commitment to build it.

## Inventory and field guide

Revision 1 contains **215 research entries**, including **64 live-dribble entries**. Three entries are explicitly excluded by owner direction: the Euro-step layup and two post-oriented actions. Counts include families, variations, chains, reactions, and supporting actions; they are not counts of unique dribble techniques or authored clips. The coverage sheet has **10 parent groups and 41 cross-cutting case records**, for 51 rows. Each move points to a valid parent coverage ID.

Priorities are **P1** for a candidate core interaction; **P2** for a candidate expanded slice; **P3** for later study; **Deferred** for a dependency outside the proposed early rules; **Excluded** for owner-rejected scope retained only as a research record. Priority does not mean a move is approved for production. Document 08, rather than every P1 label, defines the proposed first-playable selection.

The `kind` field distinguishes family, variation, sequence, reaction, transition, and presentation. A transition connects action states; presentation includes nonessential expressive motion. `entry_state` and `exit_state` describe broad prerequisites, with `review_note` identifying a specific concern. Precise frame markers and legal branches still need individual production briefs.

Coverage records are a requirements checklist. They are not the fully expanded Cartesian matrix of every hand, plant, body, outfit, pace, contact, and result. Add explicit case IDs for the selected subset during specification; mark shared, not applicable, or deferred combinations with reasons.

## Catalogue groups

- Ready stance, pivots, jabs, and other pre-dribble actions.
- Live dribble control, rhythm changes, deception, transfers, escapes, and launches.
- Multi-action sequences and advanced study candidates.
- Braking, shooting gathers, jumpers, and step-throughs.
- Layups, rim gathers, dunk families, and alley-oop finishes.
- Passing, receiving, off-ball cuts, and return-pass calls.
- Defender reactions, contacts, loose balls, and recovery.

Some of these are deliberately not dribble moves. Keeping them distinct prevents an inflated claim that every listed action is a unique dribble technique.

## Completeness requires a coverage matrix

For each selected action, enumerate applicable variations across:

| Dimension | Examples |
|---|---|
| Ball hand | Left, right, both hands, no ball |
| Movement | Stationary, forward, lateral, retreating, diagonal |
| Pace | Slow setup, normal movement, explosive exit, tired |
| Footwork | Left plant, right plant, hop, stride, pivot, one-foot takeoff, two-foot takeoff |
| Approach | Center, left lane, right lane, baseline, traffic |
| Defense | Open, late recovery, side pressure, body contact, hand contest |
| Outcome | Complete, redirected, cancelled when legal, bobble, block, miss, recovery |
| Character | Supported body proportions and dominant hand |
| Connection | Valid entry, valid next action, and fallbacks |
| Presentation | Main camera, cinematic finish, wide finish |

Do not multiply every dimension blindly. Many combinations are impossible, redundant, or better served by a shared blend. Mark each combination **required**, **shared**, **not applicable with reason**, or **deferred**. Only required combinations count toward completion.

Example planning arithmetic: eight actions × two hands × three movement contexts × two exits would produce 96 test cases before contact. It does not prove that 96 bespoke clips are needed.

## Definition of covered

An action is covered only when its applicable entry states, hand/foot contacts, branches, intended result, failure path, recovery, and camera visibility are specified and approved. Later production adds asset existence and device validation. Presently, none of the animation families is production-complete.

Suggested lifecycle: candidate → basketball-reviewed → behavior-specified → storyboard-approved → asset-brief-ready → authored → transition-tested → device-approved. The final four steps belong to a future development phase.

## Additional study queue

Investigate less standardized handles such as outside-hand wrapping pulls, extended push-and-retrieve feints, reverse hand transfers, deceptive skip steps, and streetball tricks. Distinguish legal live-play techniques from exhibition moves that may involve carrying, double dribbling, or implausible footwork. Exhibition-only actions belong in style practice if included.

Terminology is inconsistent across coaches and communities. Add aliases to an existing family where appropriate instead of treating every nickname as a separate move. A basketball movement reviewer should resolve disputed classifications and legality.

## Remaining research limitations

The research sampled multiple eras of public control documentation and checked current public gameplay descriptions. It did not inspect every released clip, every hidden transition, every body-type variation, or a complete installed-game animation catalogue. Exact timings and thresholds have not been measured. No statement of exhaustive coverage is justified yet.

The practical target is complete coverage of **our explicitly selected game scope**, plus a maintained catalogue of future candidates. The first playable can be small while the overall animation plan stays broad.
