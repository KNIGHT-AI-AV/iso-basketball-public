# Economy and no-ad monetization

## Confirmed direction and unresolved choice

Microtransactions are required. Advertising is excluded, including optional rewarded ads. The owner wants a profitable game that does not annoy or punish players for declining purchases.

Proposed currency names are **Coins** for earned progression and **Style Credits** for paid cosmetics. These are working labels. If Style Credits can convert to Coins and Coins buy stats, the economy includes paid progression. It cannot honestly be called cosmetics-only. The conversion is an option under consideration, not a settled ratio.

## Simple ownership model

| Item | Earn or acquire | Spend on |
|---|---|---|
| Coins | Completed possessions, challenges, career milestones, practice objectives | Seven stat categories; selected cosmetics |
| Style Credits | Direct purchase; optional clearly disclosed promotional grants | Known cosmetic items and bundles; optional conversion if adopted |
| Cosmetic ownership | Buy a specific item or earn a specific reward | Permanent equip permission; no resale/trading in the initial scope |

Make the wallet understandable from one screen. Display exact balances, cost, what the player receives, and the result before confirming. Cosmetic previews should use the player's actual body, hair, tattoos, and outfit so clipping or fit differences are visible.

## Recommended fairness rules

- Sell known items. Avoid randomized purchases in the proposed initial economy.
- Keep normal play, practice, and retries available without an energy purchase.
- No ads, paid shot-clock extension, mid-attempt stamina purchase, or purchase popup after a frustrating miss.
- Keep footwear, jewelry, tattoos, clothing, barefoot, and shirtless options cosmetic. Their visual rarity does not change grip, jump height, speed, or shot tolerance.
- Do not sell an exclusive mechanically superior dunk or handle. Cosmetic execution styles must preserve the same playable opportunities.
- If paid conversion funds upgrades, recommend normalized ratings in competitive comparison modes and clear separation from progression challenges. This is a design recommendation, not an approved rule.
- Offer a useful starter wardrobe and meaningful earnable looks. Optional premium expression can be extensive without charging for basic control quality.
- Show bundle contents and ownership duplicates clearly. Avoid requiring intentionally excessive leftover currency to buy a displayed item.

The owner mentioned expensive barefoot/shirtless appearances. Treat that as a possible prestige reward or cosmetic tier, not a decided cash price. A high price needs perceived value, transparent access, and a viable earned route if promised. Do not charge separately for every routine outfit change.

## How to determine conversion fairly

Start with measured Coins per enjoyable session, the number of sessions needed for a meaningful upgrade, the useful lifetime of an upgrade, and the desired pacing of each career stage. Then simulate free and paying progression. Choose a conversion only after identifying where paid acceleration changes challenge difficulty.

Example worksheet variables: earned Coins per ten-minute session; upgrade cost by level; average possessions per session; expected win rate; cosmetic acquisition time; premium pack contents; equivalent real-money cost. None has an approved value yet. A ratio by itself cannot establish profitability or player satisfaction.

If conversion exists, use one clearly displayed rate, show the exact received Coins, and make the irreversible direction explicit. No cash-out or player trading is proposed. Earned and purchased origin should remain distinguishable internally even if the UI shows one coin balance, so support and refunds can be resolved correctly.

## Revenue sensitivity, not a forecast

Monthly gross purchase revenue = monthly active players × monthly payer share × average monthly spend per payer.

| Illustrative scenario | Active players/month | Payer share | Spend/payer/month | Gross/month | Gross/year if constant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 25,000 | 2% | $10 | $5,000 | $60,000 |
| B | 100,000 | 3% | $15 | $45,000 | $540,000 |
| C | 250,000 | 4% | $12 | $120,000 | $1,440,000 |

These inputs are invented scenarios, not market benchmarks or acquisition projections. Downloads are not monthly active players. Gross revenue is not profit or take-home income. Platform fees, taxes, refunds, development, content, support, infrastructure, and acquisition costs reduce the result. No currency ratio guarantees a million-dollar business.

Measure retention and enjoyment before maximizing spend. Track progression pace, time to first useful reward, pay/free outcome gaps, purchase regret/refunds, and repeat cosmetic use. Do not judge success solely from revenue per payer while the broader audience leaves.

## Later purchase infrastructure

An eventual release needs the applicable platform purchase flow, verified transactions, a durable authoritative ledger, idempotent grants, consistent item ownership, interrupted-purchase recovery, refund reconciliation, and account recovery. Regional billing rules and store policies must be rechecked at implementation time. Do not assume one payment rule applies in every storefront.

Offline practice should remain playable. Paid balances and newly purchased items must not be granted solely because a local client claims payment succeeded. Define offline earned-progress reconciliation before introducing cloud saves. A receipt, a credit grant, and an equipped item are separate states worth testing.

Commerce is future scope. No live store products, prices, payment credentials, or purchase systems were created in this research phase.
