## What this research established

The supplied public reel was viewable without signing in. Its portrait rear view, full-body framing, centered basket, and practice obstacle informed the camera document. The research consulted public basketball movement material, historical control documentation, current public gameplay announcements, touch-input and animation documentation, platform haptic documentation, a timing-check example, commerce policies, and naming/rights guidance. These files contain original proposals and common basketball descriptions. They omit the restricted basketball reference product's name, copied control tables, and citations as requested. No installed-game animation glossary, internal source code, extracted asset library, player data dump, or complete frame-by-frame control experiment was inspected. Do not advertise this work as complete coverage of another product or every basketball move ever performed.

## Evidence and design are different

Observed reference composition is evidence. Suggested gesture thresholds, stat caps, shot windows, school identities, pricing examples, and engine preference are design hypotheses. A named move in a CSV does not prove legal footwork, licensed motion, or a finished implementation.

The next validation priorities are basketball-specialist review of the atlas, original six-frame control storyboards, camera framing across screen shapes, and a concrete first asset brief. Phone experiments follow only when development is authorized.

## Production rights approach

Sanitized reference copies were kept privately for research and are not published with this dossier; see the [reference note](../research-references/README.md). Private storage never changed their production-asset status.

Create original characters, team identities, courts, uniforms, shoes, sounds, interface, and motion. Generic sporting ideas and movement concepts are distinct from a particular game's code, audiovisual expression, recordings, branding, or licensed athlete likenesses. That distinction is not blanket clearance for a close replica.

Do not extract animation files, reproduce proprietary package tables, copy distinctive interface art, trace branded shoes, or reuse broadcast/social footage as production material without the necessary rights. Public availability does not make a work public domain. Omitting attribution does not create permission.

Before capture or generation, confirm that the input license permits the intended commercial, derivative, training, or AI-assisted use. A license allowing personal viewing may not permit those uses. Prefer commissioned athlete footage with written releases and clearly scoped rights. Keep production asset provenance and license records; these are ownership records rather than citations in the design dossier.

For tattoos, use original licensed artwork; do not assume an athlete's real tattoo design can be copied. For shoes, design new silhouettes and branding; a different color alone does not make an existing shoe design original. Stock tools and libraries require their own license review, including redistribution limits.

## Naming status

The owner selected **ISO: Basketball** as the application name. Legal availability has not been established. The forty school identities remain working concepts, not cleared brands; their fictional intent does not establish that no real organization uses a similar name.

The proposed professional league name and abbreviation have public collision signals: multiple basketball organizations already use the abbreviation, and the full proposed phrase also appears publicly. Neither availability nor infringement has been established. Keep it a placeholder until a suitable trademark and marketplace review addresses similar names, related services/goods, geography, and overall branding.
